Airlines may suspend or cancel flights due to force majeure events, such as
armed conflict, war, civil unrest, or government-issued airspace closures.
In these cases, standard delay-compensation rules (such as EU261) typically
do not apply, since the disruption is considered outside the airline's
control. Most airlines' conditions of carriage state that if a flight is
cancelled due to war or a closed airspace, passengers are usually entitled
to a full refund or a fee-free rebooking to a later date, though policies
vary by airline and by the specific situation. During active conflict
periods, national aviation authorities may also issue temporary flight
bans to and from certain airports, which airlines are required to follow
regardless of individual ticket terms. Passengers are generally advised to
check the airline's official advisory page for conflict-related schedule
changes rather than assuming standard delay or cancellation policies apply.
